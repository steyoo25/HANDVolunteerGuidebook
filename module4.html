Foundation

Design Principles for Dementia Patients

Arts and craft activities play an important role as you volunteer with dementia patients. When making crafts for residents, keep things simple, safe, and respectful.

Dementia can affect vision, memory, language, and coordination. Small design choices matter. The goal is to create items that feel comforting and easy to use, not overwhelming.

Clarity

Many residents will have low vision or trouble processing complicated visuals. Use bold and high-contrast colors like dark text on a light background.

Comfort

Soft materials like fleece, felt, or cotton can be soothing. Items that feel nice to hold help reduce restlessness. Choose gentle textures and avoid scratchy or irritating materials.

Safety

Crafts should be sturdy and safe. Residents may drop items or handle them firmly. Avoid glass or fragile materials. Make sure everything is securely attached and smooth to touch.

Print

Always print messages clearly in large block letters. Keep wording short and warm. Many seniors find cursive hard to read, and simple language is more welcoming.

⭐
The Heart of Your Work

Your thoughtful design shows how much you care for these patients. When you prioritize clarity, comfort, and safety, your craft becomes something that respects the person receiving it and gives you the opportunity to engage with them more deeply.

Craft 1: "Thinking of You" Caring Cards

1

Caring Cards

High-quality, handmade cards with uplifting messages

Goal: Create high-quality, handmade cards with uplifting messages for residents to keep in their rooms.

Materials Needed

  • Heavy cardstock
  • Stamps
  • Stickers
  • Envelopes

Instructions

  1. The Front: Create light, cheerful designs such as flowers, sunshine, or birds. Avoid dark or "scary" imagery.
  2. The Message: Write a warm, general greeting like: "Sending you the brightest wishes and hoping you have a wonderful day!"
  3. The Signature: Sign as: "Your friends from the Duke HAND Club"
Pro Tip: Do not mention holidays or specific dates so the cards can be used for any event year-round.

Craft 2: Sensory "Sunshine" Goodie Bags

2

Sensory Goodie Bags

Small bags filled with items that stimulate the senses

Goal: Create small bags filled with items that stimulate the senses, including smell, touch, and sight.

Materials Needed

  • Small paper or fabric bags
  • Ribbons
  • Various sensory fillers (see below)

What to Include in Each Bag

👁

Visual

Include a bright, hand-decorated bookmark or a printed photo of a peaceful nature scene.

👐

Tactile

Add a small "fidget" item such as a smooth painted rock or a soft felt flower.

👃

Olfactory

Include a small lavender sachet or lightly scented lotion. Ensure it is skin-friendly.

Pro Tip: If including treats, stick to soft, familiar candies that are not a choking hazard.

Craft 3: No-Sew Fleece Lap Blankets

3

Fleece Blankets

Warm, comforting blankets with no sewing required

Goal: Provide warmth and a sense of security during long events.

Materials Needed

  • Two 1.5-yard pieces of fleece (matching or contrasting colors)
  • Fabric scissors

Instructions

  1. Prep: Layer the two pieces of fleece and cut a 4 x 4 inch square out of each corner.
  2. Fringing: Cut 1-inch wide strips about 4 inches deep along all four sides.
  3. Tying: Tie the top and bottom fringes together in a double knot.
Pro Tip: Choose nostalgic patterns such as florals or classic plaids that might spark memories.

Craft 4: Popsicle Stick Photo Frames

4

Photo Frames

Simple, sturdy frames for family photos or calming images

Goal: Create simple, sturdy photo frames that residents can use to display family photos or calming images.

Materials Needed

  • Wide craft sticks (popsicle sticks)
  • Strong craft glue or hot glue (adult supervision required)
  • Foam shapes or large pom-poms
  • Large buttons (securely attached)
  • Printed photos (4x4 or 4x6)
  • Cardstock backing
  • Magnets or ribbon (optional for hanging)

Instructions

  1. Build Frame: Glue 4 craft sticks into a square or rectangle. Reinforce the back with an extra stick. Let it dry completely.
  2. Add Photo Backing: Glue cardstock to the back on three sides, leaving the top open to slide in a photo (or glue photo securely in place).
  3. Decorate: Add large foam shapes or pom-poms to corners only. Keep colors bright and limit decorations.
  4. Display: Attach ribbon, a back stand, or a magnet if desired. Ensure all pieces are securely glued and edges are smooth.
Pro Tip: Insert a peaceful nature image if family photos are not available. Landscapes with clear skies, gardens, or beaches work especially well.
